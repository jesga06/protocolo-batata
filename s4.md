# Section 4 - Enemies & Drops

## 4.1 Overview

Enemies are the primary source of challenge throughout the game.

Rather than relying on complex AI, enemies are designed around simple behaviors combined with distinct strengths and weaknesses.

The goal is to overwhelm the player through numbers, variety and pressure rather than intelligence.

Each enemy should be understandable within a few seconds of first encounter.

---

# 4.2 General Enemy Behavior

Unless otherwise specified, every enemy follows the same basic behavior:

1. Spawn outside the visible screen.
2. Acquire the player's position.
3. Move directly toward the player.
4. Attempt to damage the player by contact.
5. Die when HP reaches zero.
6. Drop loot (if applicable).

Enemies do **not**:

* dodge projectiles;
* avoid each other;
* predict player movement;
* flank;
* search for cover;
* retreat.

This simplicity is intentional.

---

# 4.3 Enemy Statistics

Every enemy instance contains at minimum:

* Current HP
* Maximum HP
* Movement Speed
* Contact Damage
* Damage Cooldown
* XP Value
* Loot Table
* Current Position
* Current Movement Direction
* Active Status Effects

Future versions may extend this structure without modifying existing gameplay systems.

---

# 4.4 Enemy Scaling

Enemy difficulty increases naturally throughout a run.

Scaling may affect:

* number of enemies per wave;
* enemy spawn frequency;
* probability of stronger enemy archetypes;
* total enemies simultaneously alive.

Scaling should remain smooth.

The player should feel constant pressure rather than sudden difficulty spikes.

---

# 4.5 Spawn System

Enemies spawn outside the visible game area.

Spawn locations should be randomized while ensuring:

* enemies never appear inside the player's field of view;
* enemies never spawn directly on top of the player;
* enemy distribution feels reasonably uniform around the arena.

The arena itself is considered effectively infinite.

Only the area surrounding the player is relevant.

---

# 4.6 Enemy Waves

Enemies spawn in continuous waves.

Over time:

* wave size increases;
* spawn delay decreases;
* stronger archetypes become more common.

The increase should be gradual rather than divided into explicit stages.

# 4.7 Standard Enemy

Purpose:

Baseline enemy.

Behavior:

Walk directly toward the player.

Statistics:

* Medium HP
* Medium Speed
* Medium Damage

Abilities:

None.

Flavor Text:

> **Intern**
>
> *"Fresh from orientation. Still dangerous."*

---

# 4.8 Tank Enemy

Purpose:

Absorb damage.

Behavior:

Walk directly toward player.

Statistics:

* Very High HP
* Slow Speed
* High Damage
* Long Damage Cooldown

Weakness:

Easy to kite.

Flavor Text:

> **Industrial Refrigerator**
>
> *"Not fast. Not subtle. Still coming."*

---

# 4.9 Fast Enemy

Purpose:

Pressure mobility.

Behavior:

Walk directly toward player.

Statistics:

* Very Low HP
* High Speed
* Low Damage
* Short Damage Cooldown

Weakness:

Dies quickly.

Flavor Text:

> **Caffeine Addict**
>
> *"Consumed twelve energy drinks and bad decisions."*

---

# 4.10 Phasing Enemy

Purpose:

Interrupt player rhythm.

Behavior:

Walk toward player.

Every few seconds enters Phase State.

During Phase State:

* semi-transparent sprite;
* immune to all damage;
* continues moving normally.

After phase ends:

Returns to normal.

Flavor Text:

> **Warranty Void**
>
> *"Collision sold separately."*

---

# 4.11 Bulletproof Enemy

Purpose:

Encourage melee combat.

Behavior:

Standard movement.

Passive Ability:

Immune to ranged damage.

Receives damage only from melee attacks.

Future balancing may allow explosive damage.

Flavor Text:

> **Ballistic Enthusiast**
>
> *"Read the manual. Ignored the bullets."*

---

# 4.12 Ranged Enemy

This archetype only appears if the corresponding gameplay configuration is enabled.

Behavior:

Attempts to maintain distance.

Periodically fires projectiles toward the player.

Statistics:

* Medium HP
* Low Movement Speed
* Medium Projectile Damage

Projectiles:

Travel in straight lines.

Disappear after a fixed lifetime.

Flavor Text:

> **Patch Notes**
>
> *"The enemies have learned geometry."*

---

# 4.13 Future Enemy Archetypes

The system should allow additional archetypes without changing the spawning architecture.

Examples:

* explosive enemy;
* healer;
* summoner;
* elite variants;
* boss enemies.

---

# 4.14 Enemy Loot

Upon death, an enemy may generate loot.

Possible drops include:

* XP
* Healing
* Weapon Crate
* Equipment Crate

Certain special enemies may have increased drop chances.

Loot tables should be data-driven whenever possible.

---

# 4.15 Drop Physics

When generated:

1. Drop appears at enemy position.
2. Receives a small impulse toward a random direction.
3. Performs a short "hop".
4. Lands on the ground.
5. Begins idle animation.

Drops remain on the ground until collected.

---

# 4.16 Drop Collection

The player collects drops by touching them.

There is no interaction key.

Collection should feel immediate.

Upon collection:

* play sound effect;
* play pickup animation;
* remove object from world;
* apply corresponding effect.

---

# 4.17 Experience Drops

Appearance:

Purple wooden crate.

Purpose:

Grant experience.

Behavior:

Immediately increases player's XP.

If enough XP is collected:

Trigger Level-Up.

Flavor Text:

> *"Knowledge, conveniently packaged."*

---

# 4.18 Healing Drops

Appearance:

Red wooden crate.

White medical cross.

Effect:

Restore player HP.

Cannot exceed maximum HP.

Flavor Text:

> *"Contains approximately 70% hope."*

---

# 4.19 Weapon Crates

Appearance:

Military-style wooden crate.

Effect:

Replace current special weapon.

New weapon becomes active immediately.

Current weapon disappears.

Flavor Text:

> *"Definitely acquired legally."*

---

# 4.20 Equipment Crates

Appearance:

Orange wooden crate.

Black screwdriver icon.

Effect:

Replace current equipment.

Equipment duration resets to 30 seconds.

Flavor Text:

> *"Some assembly required."*

---

# 4.21 Configuration Unlocks

Configurations are **not** dropped by enemies.

Gameplay configurations are unlocked exclusively through the level-up system.

This keeps permanent progression tied directly to experience rather than luck.

---

# 4.22 Visual Language

Every drop shares the same wooden crate base model.

Only the color palette and icon differ.

This provides:

* visual consistency;
* lower asset production cost;
* immediate player recognition.

Players should identify drops from a glance without reading text.

---

# 4.23 Design Philosophy

Enemies are intentionally predictable.

Their role is to create pressure, not puzzles.

Challenge comes from:

* enemy combinations;
* positioning;
* increasing density;
* projectile management;
* choosing the right temporary weapons;
* selecting gameplay configurations.

Likewise, drops should create quick, instinctive decisions.

The player should rarely hesitate after recognizing a drop.

The ideal gameplay flow is:

Fight.

Collect.

Level up.

Experiment.

Repeat.

Every system in this section exists to reinforce that loop.
