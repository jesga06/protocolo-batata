# **Protocolo: Batata**

# Section 1 - Overview

## 1.1 High Concept

The game is a 2D top-down arena survival shooter inspired by games such as *Vampire Survivors*.

Unlike traditional survivors-likes, the player's primary long-term progression is not unlocking stronger weapons or passive upgrades, but unlocking **game configuration options**.

Almost every gameplay-affecting setting is initially locked.

During gameplay, the player permanently unlocks new configuration options. These configurations can later be enabled or disabled before or during any future run.

The central design philosophy is that **less comfortable settings provide gameplay advantages**.

Examples include:

* lowering the frame rate cap
* disabling the HUD
* enabling mouse-only movement
* reducing graphics quality
* using intentionally inconvenient keybinds

Instead of treating the settings menu as something outside gameplay, this game transforms it into one of the game's primary progression systems.

---

## 1.2 Elevator Pitch

> A survivors-like arena shooter where intentionally making the game harder or more inconvenient rewards the player with gameplay advantages.

The game asks a simple question:

> **"How much comfort are you willing to sacrifice for power?"**

---

## 1.3 Design Philosophy

The project follows five major principles.

### 1. Gameplay First

The game intentionally avoids unnecessary complexity.

Movement, combat and enemy AI are deliberately simple so that the configuration system becomes the main attraction.

The goal is not to overwhelm the player with mechanics, but to create interesting decisions.

---

### 2. Every Configuration Is a Decision

Changing a setting should never feel cosmetic.

Whenever possible, a configuration should have:

* an immediate gameplay consequence;
* a clear trade-off;
* a visible impact.

Players should frequently ask themselves:

> "Is this worth turning on?"

rather than

> "Which setting gives the biggest number?"

---

### 3. Simplicity Over Realism

Nothing in the game attempts to simulate reality.

If lowering graphics quality increases damage...

...then it increases damage.

No lore explanation is required.

The absurdity is intentional.

---

### 4. Permanent Discovery

Every unlocked configuration permanently expands future possibilities.

The player is not simply earning stronger numbers.

They are gradually expanding the Settings menu itself.

By the end of the game, the player has transformed what originally looked like a normal Options menu into an arsenal of gameplay modifiers.

---

### 5. Funny Without Becoming a Joke

The game should maintain a comedic tone.

Configuration descriptions, unlocks and flavor text are encouraged to be humorous.

However, gameplay should remain consistent and mechanically solid.

The joke is the premise.

The gameplay should still feel satisfying.

---

## 1.4 Target Audience

The game is intended for players who enjoy:

* survivors-like games;
* arcade action;
* experimentation;
* roguelites;
* discovering unusual mechanics.

No previous knowledge is required.

Runs should be understandable within a few minutes.

---

## 1.5 Core Gameplay Loop

A standard gameplay loop follows the structure below.

1. Start a run.
2. Spawn with only the default equipment.
3. Defeat enemies.
4. Collect XP.
5. Level up.
6. Permanently unlock a new gameplay configuration.
7. Continue surviving against increasingly difficult enemy waves.
8. Collect temporary weapons and equipment.
9. Survive until death (or defeat the boss, if implemented).

After the run ends:

* unlocked configurations remain available forever;
* temporary weapons disappear;
* temporary equipment disappears;
* the player may change active configurations before the next run.

---

## 1.6 Player Progression

The game contains **two completely separate progression systems**.

### Permanent Progression

Permanent progression exists outside gameplay.

The player permanently unlocks gameplay configurations.

These remain available forever.

Examples:

* FPS cap options
* graphics quality
* HUD toggle
* movement modifiers
* enemy modifiers
* future configuration unlocks

Permanent progression is saved locally.

---

### Temporary Progression

Temporary progression exists only during the current run.

Examples include:

* XP levels
* temporary weapons
* temporary equipment
* healing
* buffs

Everything in this category is lost when the player dies.

---

## 1.7 Game Structure

The game consists of a single arena.

There are no stages.

There are no campaigns.

There is no story progression.

Difficulty naturally increases over time through enemy spawning.

Future versions may introduce boss encounters after a certain survival time.

---

## 1.8 Core Gameplay Pillars

Every new feature added to the game should strengthen at least one of these pillars.

### Configuration Experimentation

The player should constantly try combinations of settings.

---

### Fast Combat

Combat should remain responsive and understandable.

Enemies are intentionally simple.

---

### Permanent Progression

Every run should feel meaningful because it permanently expands the player's available configurations.

---

### Chaotic Gameplay

Large numbers of enemies.

Frequent drops.

Projectiles everywhere.

Explosions.

Visual noise.

Controlled chaos.

---

### Player Choice

Whenever possible, the player should choose between:

* convenience
* power

instead of

* good
* bad

A configuration should rarely be strictly better than another.

---

## 1.9 Visual Identity

The game embraces an intentionally exaggerated relationship between performance settings and gameplay.

Players should immediately recognize that the Settings menu is not merely an options menu.

It is one of the game's core mechanics.

The game's humor comes from treating ordinary graphics options as if they were legendary RPG items.

Example flavor texts:

> **Corporate Budget Cuts**
>
> *"Textures are a luxury."*

---

> **Cinematic Experience**
>
> *"Every frame was handcrafted."*

---

> **Trust Me Bro**
>
> *"Health bars are merely suggestions."*

---

> **Keyboard Anarchy**
>
> *"Your muscle memory had a good run."*

---

## 1.10 Scope

This project intentionally prioritizes originality over complexity.

Features such as sophisticated AI, procedural worlds, online multiplayer, dialogue systems or narrative progression are outside the project's scope.

The intended experience is:

* easy to understand;
* easy to replay;
* mechanically consistent;
* humorous;
* highly configurable.

The project's defining feature is the transformation of the traditional Settings menu into the player's permanent progression system.
