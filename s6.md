# Section 6 - Art Direction, Audio & Presentation

## 6.1 Overview

The game's presentation should reinforce its main identity:

A chaotic, comedic, experimental arena shooter where ordinary computer settings become gameplay mechanics.

The visual and audio style should not aim for realism or cinematic quality.

Instead, it should focus on:

* readability;
* personality;
* low production cost;
* strong visual feedback.

The game should feel like a small, intentionally chaotic arcade machine.

---

# 6.2 Visual Style

## 6.2.1 Pixel Art Direction

The entire game uses pixel art.

Visual characteristics:

* low-resolution sprites;
* strong silhouettes;
* limited color palettes;
* exaggerated animations;
* simple shapes.

The art style should prioritize gameplay readability over detail.

A player should identify an enemy, weapon, or drop immediately while surrounded by dozens of objects.

---

# 6.2.2 Player Character

The protagonist is a small robot.

Design goals:

* recognizable silhouette;
* simple animation requirements;
* expressive despite limited detail.

Required animations:

* idle;
* walking;
* taking damage;
* attacking;
* death.

Possible additional animations:

* collecting drops;
* weapon-specific actions;
* configuration-related effects.

---

# 6.2.3 Enemy Design

Enemies should use strong visual archetypes.

A player should understand an enemy's role without reading information.

Examples:

Tank enemy:

* large body;
* slow animation;
* heavy appearance.

Fast enemy:

* small body;
* exaggerated movement.

Ranged enemy:

* visible weapon or projectile launcher.

Phasing enemy:

* transparency effect;
* visual distortion;
* color change.

---

# 6.2.4 Low Graphics Mode

Low Graphics is a gameplay configuration.

When enabled:

All entities are rendered as simple colored shapes.

Examples:

Player:

Colored rectangle.

Enemies:

Different colored rectangles.

Drops:

Colored boxes.

Effects:

Simplified particles.

The purpose is both:

* performance improvement;
* gameplay modifier.

The mode should still maintain enough visual distinction for gameplay.

---

# 6.3 Camera Presentation

The camera is top-down.

Features:

* follows player position;
* slight movement delay;
* smooth interpolation.

The camera should avoid:

* instant snapping;
* excessive shaking;
* confusing movement.

Optional effects:

* small screen shake from heavy weapons;
* damage feedback;
* explosions.

These should remain configurable if they affect accessibility.

---

# 6.4 Animation Philosophy

Animations should be simple and exaggerated.

Priority order:

1. Gameplay readability.
2. Feedback.
3. Personality.
4. Visual complexity.

Examples:

Enemy death:

* quick explosion;
* sprite distortion;
* particle burst.

Weapon firing:

* visible muzzle flash;
* projectile trail;
* recoil effect.

Damage taken:

* flash;
* sound;
* short animation.

---

# 6.5 Drop Visual Design

All drops share a wooden crate base.

The crate is intentionally generic.

The icon and color determine the drop type.

Required designs:

## XP Drop

Purple crate.

Purpose:

Experience.

---

## Weapon Drop

Military wooden crate.

Purpose:

Special weapons.

---

## Configuration Drop

Gray crate.

Golden gear symbol.

Note:

Currently reserved for future possibilities.

Gameplay configurations are unlocked through level-ups.

---

## Healing Drop

Red crate.

White medical cross.

---

## Equipment Drop

Orange crate.

Black screwdriver symbol.

---

# 6.6 Particle Effects

Particles should improve feedback.

Required effects:

* enemy death;
* weapon firing;
* explosions;
* melee attacks;
* drop collection;
* level-up.

Particles must have simplified versions for Low Graphics mode.

---

# 6.7 Audio Direction

The audio style follows a MIDI/8-bit inspired direction.

The goal is energetic and nostalgic rather than realistic.

Inspiration:

* arcade games;
* retro soundtracks;
* modern electronic music influences.

---

# 6.8 Music

The game uses one looping background track.

Requirements:

* seamless loop;
* energetic;
* supports long play sessions;
* does not become distracting.

The music should match increasing chaos as enemy density rises.

Possible implementation:

* dynamic volume changes;
* additional layers;
* intensity variation.

These are optional.

---

# 6.10 Audio Configuration

Audio settings:

Available immediately.

Options:

* Master Volume;
* Music Volume;
* SFX Volume.

These settings do not affect gameplay.

Unlike gameplay configurations, they exist purely for usability.

---

# 6.11 Humor Through Presentation

The game's comedy should appear through:

* configuration names;
* flavor texts;
* absurd consequences;
* UI messages;
* sound design.

The humor should come from treating ridiculous situations seriously.

Example:

Unlock message:

> "Achievement unlocked: You made the game worse on purpose."

# 6.13 Presentation Goals

The final presentation should create three impressions:

## First Impression

"This is a simple retro shooter."

---

## Second Impression

"Wait, why are the settings menu options affecting gameplay?"

---

## Third Impression

"I intentionally made the game run terribly and somehow became stronger."

The art and audio exist to support this progression of discovery.

The game does not need expensive assets.

It needs a consistent identity.
